一

What we builtWhat did you build?

先生 — an AI Japanese-teaching avatar on the Perxona Connect Kit. The avatar leads short lessons across nine topics — greetings, numbers, family, food, time & days, shopping, directions, self-introduction, plus a dedicated vocabulary deck — then tests what was covered in a 14-question multiple-choice quiz.

Every lesson card, quiz question and answer option is a tap. The student never has to type kana they can't yet input; a free-text box stays open underneath for anyone who wants to ask something outside the script.

二

Who it's forWho is it for?

English-speaking absolute beginners in Japanese — someone with no IME for kana, no muscle memory for the keyboard layout, and five spare minutes rather than a course to enrol in. The learner who wants a patient teacher on a phone screen, not an app that assumes they can already type what they're trying to learn.

三

The problemWhat problem does it solve?

Typing is the wrong interface for a script you don't know

Most lesson apps ask the student to type an answer in the language they're still learning to write. This one answers everything — topic, quiz choice, quick reply — with a single tap.

A text-only tutor has no presence

It can explain a word, but it can't celebrate a right answer or soften a wrong one the way a person in the room would. The avatar reacts in character to every turn — see 四, right.

Multi-lingual speech synthesis mispronounces exactly what a language app needs

A bare kanji read with Chinese pronunciation, an English sentence dragged into a Japanese accent because it shared one utterance with a Japanese word, even a stray escaped quote mark read aloud as "backslash." Each surfaced during build and each now has a specific, tested fix in the code.

四

What makes it differentBe specific

Emoji, not stock photos
Every card pairs a word with an emoji picture on purpose — instant, unambiguous, zero network calls, zero licensing to check. A downloaded illustration is one more thing that can fail live on stage; an emoji can't.
Graded emotional feedback
Right answer → thumbs-up gesture, joy at high intensity. Wrong answer → a gentle head-shake, a caring tone, then the correct answer — never disappointment aimed at the student. The reaction is chosen per outcome, not scripted once for the whole quiz.
A fixed curriculum, not a model's best guess
Lesson content, vocabulary and quiz questions are a hand-built deck, not generated per session. Every run of the demo behaves identically, and nothing depends on a model remembering a formatting instruction mid-quiz.
Three real TTS bugs, fixed at the source
山 read as Chinese → speak the hiragana, never the bare kanji. English dragged into a Japanese accent → one language per utterance, never mixed. "\"" read aloud as "backslash" → no straight quotes anywhere in spoken text.
A fork, not a rewrite
Built directly on the Connect Kit's own Embed template, zero framework — so anything Perxona ships next for the kit lands in this project for free.
